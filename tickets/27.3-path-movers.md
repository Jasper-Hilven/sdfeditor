# 27.3 — Path movers: a looping moving platform on a timetable (generic)

Goal: a level can hold a vehicle that follows a closed path on a fixed timetable from the round clock, with colliders that move, and the first-person
character controller can stand on it and be carried. The toy train (27.10) is the first user; the kitchen's and the computer's moving things reuse it.

Steps:
1. Pure rules in `SdfBattle.Core` (`PathTimetable` or similar, EditMode tests): a closed path (points, closed), its length, `PositionAt(arcLength)` with
   heading and height, a vehicle of N cars of given lengths and couplings following the path's centre line, `distance = speed * roundClock` (no saved state),
   a phase offset. Tests: the lap period, cars keep their spacing round the loop, heading, wrap-around.
2. Level file: a `mover` section (document it in the `LevelFile` header comment): `mover train speed=7` with the path as a stadium helper
   (`Stadium center=(0, 0) halfLength=12 radius=20`; the toy box needs it) or a Polyline, heights along the path as (arc length, y) keys or a bridge list,
   and an ordered list of car models with a gap. The bake writes it into `level.json`; tests for parse, errors and bake.
3. Unity runtime: a `PathMover` MonoBehaviour (Assets/Level/Runtime or Battle/Runtime) that places each car from the round clock (the `Match` clock; when
   no match runs, `Time.time`), cars with kinematic colliders so the physics sees their velocity. `LevelBuilder` creates it from the baked data, each car's
   convex collider from its model.
4. Moving platform in `FirstPersonController`: when standing on a collider that belongs to a `PathMover` car, add the platform's per-frame displacement (and
   yaw change) to the player's motion, so a player on it is carried (also a flag carrier); no change when not on one. EditMode test of the displacement maths
   if it is a pure function.
5. The train models come with 27.5: for this ticket use a throwaway level or a test fixture with placeholder boxes (not committed).

Check: `dotnet build`, `dotnet test` and the SdfBattle EditMode tests pass; meadow, fungal and chessboard bake and build unchanged. Acceptance: a mover
declared in a level file bakes and builds, cars follow the timetable, a player standing on one is carried. Dependencies: none.

Common rules (every subticket of 27): read `tickets/27-toy-box-level.md`, `tickets/README.md` and `tickets/done/26-level-ruleset.md` first. Work only in this
worktree (the absolute path the lead gives you), never the main checkout. Meshing always at 256 cells, 3 LODs (castle parts 128), never 512. Each SDF pointer

in git. Match surrounding code style and comment density. Do not commit; the lead commits. Do not spawn agents. 27's open questions are answered with its
defaults: the train pushes and does 20 damage, riding is in, the machines keep the scouts' floating eyes, the teddy and jester have painted toy faces. Report:
files changed, commands run, test results (failures stated honestly), what is left for a human look.

## Status

Done in code, tested; left for a human look.

What was done:
- Pure rules in `SdfBattle.Core` (`Unity/Assets/Battle/Core/PathTimetable.cs`): `ClosedPath` (corners, length, wrapping `PointAt(arc)`, cyclic linear `HeightAt`, `PoseBetween(rear, front)`),
  `PathTimetable` (cars of given lengths and a gap behind the head; `head = speed * (clock + phase)`, no saved state; `PoseOf(car, clock)` puts a rigid car on the chord between its rear and
  front points, so it cuts bends, with yaw and slope pitch) and `PlatformCarry` (the displacement and yaw change of a point standing on a platform from one pose to the next).
  `MatchState.RoundClock` added (seconds since the round began, through sudden death, reset each round).
- Level file: `mover <name> speed= [gap=0.5] [phase=0]` (repeatable, names unique) with below it the path `Stadium center= halfLength= radius=` (straights along z at x = centre +- radius,
  anticlockwise, arc length 0 at the east straight's south end) or `Polyline points=[...]` (closed), optional `heights keys=[(arc, y), ...]` (y above 0; no bridge list, keys do it),
  and `car <model> length=` lines head first. Documented in the `LevelFile` header; `LevelDefinition.Movers`, `MoverSpec`/`MoverCar` in `SDFPipeline/Levels/LevelMover.cs` (also the stadium maths).
  Errors: no path or cars, two paths, fewer than three corners, keys off the path or not increasing, a train longer than the loop. The bake writes `movers` into `level.json` (only when
  there are any, so the three existing levels bake unchanged), checks the car models exist and the path is on the island, and lists the car models in `models`.
- Unity: `PathMover` (clock = `MatchState.RoundClock` while a match runs, else `Time.time`; places each car every frame, execution order -50, then `Physics.SyncTransforms`) and
  `PathMoverCar` (own file, as Unity needs one MonoBehaviour per file; keeps the step it took, `Carry(standing, out yaw)`, ignores a step over 6 m so a new round does not throw anyone).
  `LevelBuilder` (version 21) builds a `Movers` root at the scene root (outside `Level`, so the bots' navmesh leaves it out): per car an empty object with a kinematic Rigidbody and
  `PathMoverCar` over the model's prefab, every mesh collider made convex (a box round the renderers, with a warning, if the model has none); path x mirrored as placements are.
  A mover whose car prefab is missing is left out with a warning.
- `FirstPersonController`: when the last move ended on a `PathMoverCar` (hit with normal.y >= 0.45, last frame, grounded), the car's step moves the player and turns the body with it
  (`Ride()`); on anything else nothing changes. Bots use the same controller, so they are carried too if they stand on a car.
