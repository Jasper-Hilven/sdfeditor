# 31.5 — Capture core and the comic post pass

Subticket of [31 — Story intro](31-story-intro.md). Goal: an editor tool that renders a panel's 3D stage with the heavy comic look, no lettering yet.

Steps:
1. `StoryCapture` (Unity editor, `Unity/Assets/Story/Editor/`): Tools > SDF Story > Render Panels and a batchmode `-executeMethod`, as
   `GuiSnapshot`; an optional panel number renders only that one. Reads `content/story/intro.txt` (31.4), opens the stage (`Home.unity`;
   `Meadow.unity` for arrival panels with nothing added but the scouts; the warp stage comes in 31.7), poses the camera, actors and
   props, renders at 3840 x 2160 and downsamples to 1920 x 1080 into `Assets/Story/Panels/panel-NN.jpg` (no mipmaps, compressed for the
   web by the importer settings the script applies; the whole set about 6 MB or less).
2. Heavier toon at capture time only: a capture-only global (shader keyword or global property) that gives 2-3 hard bands, a stronger
   rim and about 2x outline width on `SdfToon` / `SdfToonOutline`, with no change to the look in game when it is off. Check the shaders
   still build for WebGL2.
3. Post pass shader and script in `Assets/Story/Editor/` (editor only, never ships): wobbly ink line from depth and normal edges,
   halftone dots on a 45 degree grid (about 6 px at 1080p) filling the shadow band in a darker shade of the surface colour (get the band
   from a second camera pass writing the band index, not from colour guessing), warm paper grain and slight posterisation.
4. Screen material (editor only): unlit, texture, scanlines, glitch amount (block offset, blue tint, tear), soap-film bulge as a vertex
   offset by a uniform. Used by the monitor's `Screen` part.

Check: Unity compiles (batchmode `-quit` reports no errors) and panel 1 renders to a PNG you can look at; say honestly if Unity could not
run. Dependencies: 31.3, 31.4.

Common rules (every subticket of 31): read `tickets/31-story-intro.md` (the whole ticket) and `tickets/README.md` first. Work only in this
worktree, never the main checkout. Meshing always at 256 cells, 3 LODs (home models: LOD0 is enough), never 512. Each SDF pointer id is
one node (reuse only via variables). One theme per scene; toon look only; nothing in the runtime may need compute shaders (WebGL2);
HUD/GUI elements about 5 px from the screen edges. 42 is always "they", Prime is "he". Generated files are not in git. Match the
surrounding code style and comment density. Do not commit; the lead commits. Any agent you spawn must be a Sonnet agent
(`model: "sonnet"`) and must be told the same. Report: files changed, commands run, test results (failures stated honestly), what is left
for a human look.

## Status

Done; Unity ran in batchmode and rendered all twelve panels (home, meadow, warp stage) with no compile errors; panels 1, 3, 7, 10, 11 looked at. Left for a human look: the comic look
by eye (colours are pastel, the lamp and monitor pools on the walls are large hard discs) and every panel's camera and scale numbers.

- Capture-only look: `Unity/Assets/Mushrooms/Shaders/SdfComic.hlsl` (globals `_SdfComic`, `_SdfComicLightPos/Color`; zero = off, no keywords, no compute).
  `SdfToon.hlsl` takes hard band edges and a stronger, wider rim when on; `SdfToonOutline.shader` is 2x wider when on and discards in the aux pass;
  `SdfToon`, `SdfToonFar`, `SdfGround`, `SdfRoomFace` end in `SdfToonOutput` (the colour as it was when off, plus the story's lamp/monitor point lights in
  the colour pass, or normal/depth/band in the aux pass). The toon shaders only take the sun, so the room's point lights shade through this. Off, the
  maths is the same as before. Compiled for gles3, glcore, vulkan and webgpu with no new warning (checked with `ShaderUtil.OpenCompiledShader`).
- Deviation: the band for the halftone is written by a second render (the aux pass, ARGBFloat: octahedral normal, distance, band) of the same view, not the
  alpha of the colour pass (URP's HDR buffer has no alpha). The same aux gives the post pass its depth and normal edges.
- `Unity/Assets/Story/Editor/StoryCapture.cs`: Tools > SDF Story > Render Panels / Render Panel 1; batch
  `Unity -batchmode -quit -projectPath Unity -executeMethod SdfStory.Editor.StoryCapture.CaptureInBatch [-panel 1[,5,7]] [-panelDir <dir>]` (no -nographics;
  about 15 s for a panel plus the first import). Per panel: opens the stage (Home.unity; Meadow.unity after `LevelBuilder.BuildStale`; an empty lit stage
  for the warp, the tunnel is 31.7), forces LOD0, poses actors (whole = absolute in the pipeline frame with x mirrored and yaw negated; part = change on the
  rest pose; body = the scout's child that is neither eye nor hand; left = the lower Unity x), the lights and the monitor's `screen`; renders 3840 x 2160 in
  the two passes, runs the post pass, averages down to 1920 x 1080 and writes `Assets/Story/Panels/panel-NN.jpg` (import: no mipmaps, crunch, 2048). Scouts
  missing from the stage (meadow, warp) are made from the `amanita-scout` / `chanterelle-scout-hand0|1` prefabs. Inset frames get a plain 5 px ink line and a
  paper gutter (the irregular border, devices and lettering are 31.6). Camera `fov` is taken as vertical, roll as Unity's z (positive = horizon clockwise).
- `StoryComicPost.shader` (Hidden/SdfStory/ComicPost): wobbly ink line from depth and normal jumps, 45 degree halftone (6 px at 1080p) in the shadow band in a
  darker, more saturated shade of the colour, warm paper tint, 2 px grain and a slight posterise. `StoryScreen.shader` (SdfStory/Screen): unlit texture,
  scanlines, glitch (block rows, tear, colour split, blue blocks, tint) and the soap-film bulge as a vertex offset on a 96 x 54 grid that the capture puts in
  place of the 12-triangle slab (a slab has nothing to push). Without `Assets/Story/Screens/<name>.png` (31.7) a stand-in picture with a red top-left corner
  is used.
- Data: `content/story/intro.txt` panel 1 gets one line, `actor "Mushroom 42" pos=(-0.5, 0.5, -0.45) rot=(0, 180, 0) scale=0.7`. The scouts are 1.6 m tall (cap
  top 1.62) and the furniture is made for about 1 m: at scale 1, 42 on the chair is far taller than the chair back and the monitor. The other panels were not
  tuned: panel 2's camera assumes full size, and 31.7 must settle one scale for 42 (0.7 here), the camera numbers, and the meadow cameras (panel 11's camera
  stands inside the castle's courtyard walls, not on the field). `dotnet test --filter Story`: 40 + 34 passed.
