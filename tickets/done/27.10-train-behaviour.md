# 27.10 — The toy train's behaviour

Goal: on top of 27.3's `PathMover` the train blocks, knocks scouts aside, kills as "Toy train", gives off puffs, and bots cross its track safely.

Steps (27 "Gameplay twist" and "Bots and the navmesh"):
1. Pure rules in `SdfBattle.Core` with EditMode tests: knock-aside (a scout or tank overlapping a moving car is pushed 3 m off the track sideways, at most once per
   car per second, takes 20 damage; flags, pods and turrets ignore the train); the "Toy train" damage source (no weapon, no kill credit, no spores, a kill-feed line
   "Toy train" naming the victim; read `Damage.cs`, `Death*.cs`, `DeathLines.cs`, `Combat*.cs`).
2. Unity: a trigger or sweep on each car that applies the rules to `Player`, bots and tanks; the kill-feed line; a chimney puff every second and a whistle puff 2 s
   before each bridge (a small toon puff in the existing effects' style, nothing needing compute); a dropped flag on the track stays where it lies.
3. Bots: each car carries a carving `NavMeshObstacle` (both agent types) while moving; a "crossing" check in `Bot` / `BotNavigation`: a bot whose path crosses the track
   within 25 m of the train ahead waits at the track's edge until the car has passed. Tanks steer round it like players. The pure part (is the train within 25 m of the
   crossing, when will it pass) goes in Core with tests.
4. Wire these through the level data (`mover` options such as `pushes=3 damage=20 name="Toy train"`), generic enough that another level could name its mover, not
   hard-coded to the toy box.

Check: SdfBattle EditMode tests (in Unity batch with `-runTests -testPlatform EditMode` if feasible; `cp -cR` the main checkout's `Unity/Library` first), `dotnet test`.
Acceptance: the rules are tested; the runtime builds with no `error CS`. Dependencies: 27.3, 27.5, 27.9.

## Status

Done in code and tested; left for a human look in Play mode (nothing here was run in a scene: the worktree has no meshed models, so the train was not built in Unity).

What was done:
- Pure rules in `SdfBattle.Core` (`MoverRules.cs`): `MoverImpact` (name, push distance, damage, cooldown; `Affects` = scouts and tanks only, flags, pods, turrets and helicopters ignore it; `PushedTo` puts a victim `distance` metres past the car's side on the side it is on, same place along the track and height, never pulled back in; `DamageFrom`), `MoverHitLog` (a car hits the same victim at most once per cooldown; a clock that goes back forgets), `MoverCues` (chimney ticks, whistle `lead` seconds before each climb on every lap) and `MoverCrossing` (`Check`/`CheckWalk`: wait while the head is within `warn` m before the crossing, or the train plus a 2 m margin is on it; seconds until clear). `ClosedPath` got `ClimbStarts`, `Crossings(segment)` and `DistanceTo`.
- The "Toy train" damage source: `Damage.ByHazard(name, amount, from)` / `Damage.Neutral` (no team, no weapon, no player). `MatchState.CanHurt(victim, Damage)` and `CombatState.CanHurt(team, Damage)` let a neutral hit hurt either team (still not in the countdown, not the dead or protected); its `Death` has no killer and no `KillerTeam`, so `CountsAsKill` is false (no credit, no spores: `SporeState` only gains on `CountsAsKill` or a damage with an attacker). New `DeathKind.Hazard` (picked for a killer-less death with a name and no team) with 8 feed lines, 6 titles, 5 details (`{killer}` = "Toy train"). `MatchHud` writes a team-less killer name plain, uncoloured, with no weapon icon. `Machine.TakeDamage` uses the neutral-aware check, so tanks are hurt too.
- Level format: `mover` options `pushes=` (m), `damage=`, `name="Toy train"`, `cooldown=` (1), `puff=` (s between chimney puffs), `puffAt=(x,y,z)` (in the first car's frame), `whistle=` (s before each climb), `crossing=` (m), all off by default; parser, `MoverSpec`, bake (`pushes damage hazard cooldown puff puffAt whistle crossing` in level.json), `LevelFile` header doc, `LevelBuilder` (version 22) and `PathMover` fields. `toybox.level`: `mover train speed=7 gap=0.8 pushes=3 damage=20 name="Toy train" puff=1 puffAt=(0, 2.7, 1.7) whistle=2 crossing=25` (chimney top from the engine model).
- Unity: `MoverStrikes` (new, on each mover with a hit, after the cars are placed): overlap box round each car (colliders' local bounds + 0.1 m at the sides and ends), players and tanks only; hazard damage through `Combat.Hurt` / `Machine.TakeDamage`, then, if still alive, a push through `CharacterController.Move` (a wall stops it) or the tank's transform; a player whose feet are at the car's top (the flat wagon) rides and is left alone; only in the Playing phase. `PathMover` gives the chimney and whistle puffs (`CombatFx.TrainPuff`, pale toon puffs from the existing particle pool, no compute), and `PathMoverCar.Carve` adds a carving `NavMeshObstacle` (box round the car, 0.4 m longer at each end, carve while moving, move threshold 0.3 m) to each car. Dropped flags are triggers only, so the train does not move them, and `BotNavigation.Reachable` counts a point on a mover's track (at track height) as reachable, so a flag dropped on the track is not sent home because a car's hole is cut there.
- Bots: `BotNavigation.MustWait` (lookahead 14 m on the path's corners): if the way crosses a track whose train is near or on the crossing (`MoverCrossing`), the bot stops at the track's edge (1 m short of it) and walks on once the train has passed; a bot already on the track keeps walking. Tanks: `TankMachine.CollectOthers` adds points down both sides of nearby cars (`PathMover.AvoidPoints`) so they steer round them like players; the carved Tank navmesh does the rest and a tank that is hit is pushed.
