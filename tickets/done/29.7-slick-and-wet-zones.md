# 29.7 — Slick and wet zones: soap slicks and conducting puddles (generic zone mask)

Goal: 29's "Gameplay twist". Two 2D zones baked from level regions and read at runtime by position: no colliders, no navmesh change.

Steps:
1. Level format: a small generic `zone` section naming a region and a rule (e.g. `zone suds slick` / `zone puddle wet`, your syntax, documented in the `LevelFile` header; errors for
   unknown regions or rules). The bake writes a zone mask (a low-resolution byte image over the play area, e.g. 1 m cells, or reuse `regions.png`'s data if it can be read at
   runtime cleanly) into the level output and the zone list into `level.json`; levels without zones bake byte-identically. Tests: parse, errors, bake.
2. Core rules in `SdfBattle.Core` (pure, EditMode tests): `ZoneRules`/`ZoneMask` lookup by position; `Slick`: ground acceleration x0.25, braking x0.1, top speed x1.3 for a player (bots get
   top speed x1.2 without the slide: they follow the navmesh); `Wet`: a scout standing in a wet zone, or out of it less than 2 s ago, takes x1.5 damage from the electro gun.
3. Unity runtime: load the mask in `LevelBuilder`/the level runtime (small separate file), the player motor (`FirstPersonController`; check how friction/acceleration are done; if not one
   number, add a multiplier hook with default 1) applies the slick rules; the damage path for the electro gun applies the wet multiplier; a small `FxParticles` splash per step while
   wet (cheap, optional if the particle path is awkward; say so). Keep edits to shared files (controller, damage) small and behind "no zones = unchanged".
4. Tests: EditMode tests for the rules and the mask lookup; SDFPipeline tests for the format.

Check: `dotnet build`, `dotnet test`, Unity EditMode tests pass. Acceptance: with a throwaway level having a `suds` and a `puddle` region, the bake holds the mask, a player on the slick is
modelled by the tested rules, wet damage x1.5 is tested; other levels unchanged. Dependencies: none (29.8 writes the real regions).

Bake: `zones.bin`, one byte per 1 m cell over the play area (rows from lowest z up, x fastest; bit 0 slick, bit 1 wet), a cell takes the rules of the region drawn at its centre (later region wins, as the region map).
