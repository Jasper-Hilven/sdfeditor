# 29.1 — Bathroom room look, window light and steam (generic fog)

Goal: the bathroom's walls and ceiling, a level `fog` line, and the steam. Reuse 27.1's room looks (`room size=(150, 70, 100) walls=<mat> ceiling=<mat>`, `light=`,
`lightColor=`, `ambient=`, `LevelRoom.Looks` picking a pattern by material name, `SdfRoomPlanks.shader` as the example); kitchen and computer reuse the fog.

Steps:
1. Materials `materials/room/bathroom-wall.sdfmat` and `bathroom-ceiling.sdfmat`: white `#F6F4EE` and pale mint `#CFE6D8` 5 m tiles in a checker with a thin grout line
   (`#C8D6CF`) on the walls (from world position, ruled AA, no UVs), a plain flat white `#F7F6F2` ceiling. A new shader (e.g. `SdfRoomTiles.shader`) and an entry in
   `LevelRoom.Looks`; do not edit the planks shader. Toon bands, ink line like the other room shaders.
2. Fog: new level line `fog density=0.0075 color=#E6EEEA` (exponential squared; document in the `LevelFile` header; parse, errors, bake into `level.json`; levels without the line
   bake byte-identically). `LevelBuilder` today forces "no fog" indoors: set Unity's built-in fog from the baked line (small, separate method/file), camera clears to the fog
   colour when fog is on. `SdfToon` and `SdfToonOutline` (and the room/ground shaders that need it) take `multi_compile_fog` and mix the fog per vertex (one lerp; the outline pass
   fogs the same so ink lines do not stay sharp in the haze). Keep the shader edits minimal; levels without fog must render unchanged.
3. Window light: the bathroom uses the existing room `light=`/`lightColor=`/`ambient=` options (pitch 50 down, heading +z, `#FFF8EC`, intensity 1.15, warm ambient): no new
   code if they cover it; the window model itself comes in 29.5.
4. Steam: a small generic `FxParticles` emitter placement for a level (level-file line, e.g. `steam at=(x, y, z) size=8 rise=40` with a particle budget, documented, baked,
   built by `LevelBuilder` from the baked data): three plumes over the tub (x -15, 0, 15; y 16.5), one small over the drain (0, 0.5, -28), at most 80 particles in all, 40 on the
   low preset; plus a shower-head drip (one drop every 2 s falling from (0, 57, 49.5) with a ring on the tub water) as one more emitter kind if cheap, else leave the drip
   for 29.9 and say so. Use the existing `FxParticles`/sprite pipeline; no new shaders beyond a soft sprite if none exists.
5. Tests: parser/bake tests for `fog` and `steam` (errors, bake, other levels unchanged), content materials exist.

## Status

What was done:
- Materials `materials/room/bathroom-wall.sdfmat` (white `#F6F4EE` tile, grout `#C8D6CF` as the edge colour, 0.12 m wide) and `bathroom-ceiling.sdfmat` (flat `#F7F6F2`),
  colours written linear as the other room materials. The second tile (mint `#CFE6D8`) lives in the look, not the material.
- New shader `Unity/Assets/Level/Shaders/SdfRoomTiles.shader`: 5 m checker tiles with a ruled, anti-aliased grout line from world position (no UVs), a faint per-tile tone
  step, tiles and grout melting into their average when a tile is a few pixels, toon bands from `SdfToonShade`, a brightness factor (`_Tile.w`, 1.25 for the bathroom, as the
  bands turn a white face grey), tile size 0 = one plain colour (the ceiling). `LevelRoom.Look` gained `Tiled`, `Tile`, `TileAlt`, `TileToneStep`, `TileBrightness`;
  `LevelRoom.Looks` has `bathroom-wall` and `bathroom-ceiling`; `PlanksMaterial` picks the tiles shader for a tiled look. The planks shader's look is untouched.
- Level file (documented in the `LevelFile` header, new file `SDFPipeline/Levels/LevelFx.cs` holds the records and the bake documents): `fog density=0.0075
  color=(0.902, 0.933, 0.918)` (one per level; density 0.0005 to 0.05; the colour is a 0-1 tuple, not hex, as `#` starts a comment in the format), `steam at=(x, y, z)
  size=8 rise=40 count=22` (any number; all counts together at most 80, an error names the line that would pass it) and `drip at=(0, 57, 49.5) water=16.5 every=2 size=0.15`.
  Baked into `level.json` as `fog`, `steam`, `drips`, left out when absent: the chessboard's and toy box's `level.json` bake byte-identical (cmp against the main checkout's).
- Unity fog: new `LevelAtmosphere.cs` (editor): `ApplyFog` sets exponential-squared fog from the block after `BuildLighting` (which still turns it off); `LevelBuilder` calls it,
  the room camera clears to the fog colour (black without fog), `BuilderVersion` 23. `SdfToon`, `SdfToonOutline`, `SdfGround`, `SdfRoomFace`, `SdfRoomPlanks` and the new tiles
  shader take `multi_compile_fog` in their forward pass, compute `ComputeFogFactor` per vertex and `MixFog` after `SkyFade` (the outline pass fogs the same). Without the fog
  keyword it compiles to nothing, so levels without fog render as before. `SdfToonFar` (the far-field shader) was not touched.
- Window light: no new code; the existing `light=`, `lightColor=`, `ambient=` room options cover it (pitch 50 from +z is `light=(0, 0.77, 0.64)`).
- Steam and drip: new runtime `Level/Runtime/LevelSteam.cs` (one object at the scene root, built by `LevelAtmosphere.AddSteam`), emitting into the match's `FxParticles` pool
  (`Combat.Current.Fx.Particles`, so it only runs in a match): soft `Spore` discs (no new shader), each plume keeps `count` puffs alive (half on the low preset), rising `rise`
  m at 2.5 m/s, primed so the haze is there from the first frame. The drip is a `Drop` falling under gravity every 2 s with a ring of 8 spreading motes where it lands.
