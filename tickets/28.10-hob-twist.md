# 28.10 — The hob heats up

Goal: the gameplay twist of 28 ("Gameplay twist: the hob heats up"): burner pairs heat in turns, damage, glow, bot avoidance, kill-feed cause. Rules in `SdfBattle.Core` (pure C# with EditMode tests), a
thin runtime piece in Unity, switched on by a level-file section so it is generic and the other levels are untouched.

Steps:
1. Level format: a `heat`/hazard section (name your choice, documented in the `LevelFile` header) listing rings (centre, radius, pair group), the cycle (45 s; back pair warms 3 s, hot 12 s, cools 3 s, then the front pair,
   9 s cold between; first heat 20 s into a round), damage per second (10), cause text "the hob"; baked into `level.json`. Tests: parse, errors, bake. Add it to `kitchen.level`.
2. Core: a `HeatCycle` state machine (phase of each pair at a time `t`) and damage rule with tests: mirrored pairs hot together, the kettle position is never a ring, cooling rings do not hurt, a scout or tank
   standing in a hot ring takes 10/s, kills are credited to nobody and the cause "the hob" appears in the kill feed and the death screen (find where "fell" is produced and add the cause alongside).
3. Unity: the burner models (28.5) get the heat value written to their per-vertex parameter or a material property from the cycle (ember glow while warming, orange to yellow while hot, a heat shimmer is optional and WebGL2-safe;
   never scarlet); a NavMesh area `Burner` per ring with a runtime area cost x50 for bots and tanks while warming or hot, so they path round and step off; one component made by the match/builder, no scene setup by hand.
   If no burner shader route exists, add a small `Level/Burner` shader (URP, toon-compatible, no compute).
4. Bots do not stand on hot rings (EditMode test of the cost logic; the Play-mode check is left to a human).
